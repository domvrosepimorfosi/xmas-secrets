12 Facts of Christmas

1. The Christmas Tree Tradition

Christmas trees were not always popular in the UK or the USA. The tradition actually started in Germany in the 16th century. It became famous in the English-speaking world in 1848, when Prince Albert, the husband of Queen Victoria, put up a tree in Windsor Castle. A drawing of the Royal Family standing around the tree was published in a newspaper, and suddenly, everyone wanted one!

2. "Jingle Bells" in Space

"Jingle Bells" holds a special record. On December 16, 1965, it became the first song ever played in space. Two astronauts on the Gemini 6 spaceship played the song on a small harmonica and bells. Also, the song was originally written for American Thanksgiving, not Christmas!

3. Rudolph Was a Marketing Trick

Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer is not part of the original folklore. He was created in 1939 by a copywriter for a department store called Montgomery Ward. They created the character for a free coloring book to give to children as a promotion.

4. The Meaning of "Xmas"

Writing "Xmas" is not lazy or disrespectful. In the Greek language, the letter "X" (Chi) is the first letter of the name "Christ." Using "X" as an abbreviation for Christ is a religious tradition that has been used by the church for hundreds of years.

5. KFC in Japan

In the UK and USA, people usually eat turkey. But in Japan, the most popular Christmas dinner is Fried Chicken. In 1974, KFC started a marketing campaign called "Kentucky for Christmas." Today, millions of Japanese families order fried chicken for their holiday meal weeks in advance.

6. The Origin of Stockings

Why do we hang socks (stockings) by the fire? The legend says that St. Nicholas wanted to help a poor family. He dropped bags of gold down their chimney. The gold fell into the girls' stockings, which were hanging by the fire to dry.

7. Spiders Bring Good Luck

In most places, people do not like spiders. However, in Ukraine, spider webs are traditional Christmas decorations! There is an old story about a poor widow who could not afford decorations. A spider spun a beautiful web on her tree, which turned into gold on Christmas morning. Today, putting a spider ornament on the tree is considered good luck.

8. The First Christmas Card

Sending cards is a big part of the holiday. This tradition began in England in 1843. A civil servant named Sir Henry Cole was too busy to write individual letters to all his friends. He asked an artist to design a card with a festive image, printed a thousand copies, and sent them instead.

9. A Gift from Norway

Every year, a huge Christmas tree is put up in Trafalgar Square in London. This tree is not British; it is a gift from the people of Oslo, Norway. They send the tree every year to say "thank you" to the UK for their help during World War II.

10. Santa’s Red Suit

Many people believe that Santa Claus wears red because of Coca-Cola. This is partly true. Before the 1930s, Santa was often drawn wearing green, brown, or blue clothes. Coca-Cola hired an artist named Haddon Sundblom to draw Santa for their advertisements. He drew Santa in a bright red suit to match the brand's colors, and this image became the standard version we know today.

11. "Silent Night" Was an Accident

"Silent Night" is the most recorded Christmas song in history. It was written in Austria in 1818. The story says that the church organ was broken (some say mice ate the insides!). Because they had no organ music for the Christmas service, the priest wrote a simple song that could be played on a guitar.

12. Christmas Crackers

Christmas Crackers are a very British tradition. They were invented by a sweet-maker named Tom Smith. He saw "bon-bons" (sweets wrapped in paper) in France and wanted to sell them in London. Later, he added a small explosive "bang," a paper hat, and a bad joke to the package.
